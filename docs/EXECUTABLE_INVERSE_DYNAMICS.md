# From products to processes: executable inverse dynamics

## Scientific question

MechET is not a Qwen-specific retrosynthesis recipe.  It asks a broader
question about scientific inverse problems:

> When an answer is generated by a constrained process, should a model predict
> the answer directly, or an executable process that produces it?

One-step retrosynthesis is a stringent instance of this question because
electron, bond and valence bookkeeping make intermediate decisions executable
and falsifiable.  A sequence foundation model, graph policy or hybrid agent can
parameterize the policy; the prediction space and transition contract are the
methodological objects of interest.

## Formalization

Endpoint models learn

\[
\widehat R=\arg\max_R p(R\mid P),
\]

where `P` is the product and `R` is a precursor mixture.  Executable inverse
dynamics instead learns a program

\[
\widehat\tau=\arg\max_\tau p(\tau\mid P),\qquad
S_{t+1}=\mathcal T(S_t,a_t),\qquad
\widehat R=\mathcal D(S_T).
\]

The policy selects the inverse electron-flow event `a_t`; the deterministic
operator `T` validates and applies it; the endpoint decoder `D` reads only the
executed terminal state.  There is no separately generated answer in the
scored path.  This distinguishes three computational contracts:

1. direct endpoint prediction;
2. open-loop transformation serialization;
3. closed-loop executable inverse dynamics.

## Questions exposed by the change of prediction space

The paper is organized around observations made possible by this formulation,
not around promotion of one model architecture.

1. **Answer quality, executability and transformation fidelity are distinct.**
   A syntactically valid program may be chemically invalid; a formally
   executable program may reach a different endpoint; distinct programs may
   also compile to the same chemical state.
2. **Coordination controls difficulty.**  The relevant complexity variable is
   the number of electron transfers that must be selected jointly, not only
   SMILES length or atom count.
3. **Causal participants differ from experimental context.**  Fragments that
   donate or accept electrons are part of the transformation; recorded
   solvents, counterions, ligands and work-up species can be underdetermined by
   the product and must be analyzed separately.
4. **Execution creates process-level learning signals.**  Environment-owned
   states and errors make intermediate decisions auditable and support
   execution-grounded post-training instead of outcome-only credit assignment.
5. **Reaction knowledge can be local and compositional.**  Complete programs
   may be unseen even when their electron-flow primitives and short motifs are
   supported by training.

## Evidence sequence

1. Freeze SFT lineage and evaluate the same local decisions at the final
   checkpoint.
2. Run product-only closed-loop validation with the real executor.
3. Separate electron-participating imports from endpoint-only context.
4. Report endpoint recovery, execution and endpoint--program consistency
   together.
5. Compare SFT and execution-grounded post-training under the same candidates,
   ranking rule and denominator.
6. Analyze electron-flow coordination, reaction-center complexity, program
   novelty and qualitative correction trajectories.

Formal execution establishes symbolic consistency under the declared operator;
it does not by itself establish kinetics, yield, selectivity or laboratory
feasibility.
